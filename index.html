01 Understand

What breast cancer is.

Abnormal cells in breast tissue grow out of control.

Who can develop it

Anyone, including men, although it is far more common in women.

99%Women
1%Men
Source: GPnotebook

Know your risk factors

Age, family history and inherited genes help guide when to start checks. Knowing them lets you plan ahead.

Can risk be reduced?

Yes, often it can. Staying active, keeping a healthy weight and limiting alcohol all help.

WHO fact sheet
02 Know the signs

Five changes to take seriously.

At a glance. This site cannot diagnose.

New lump or thickening

In the breast or armpit. Many lumps are benign, but only a professional can tell.

Change in size or shape

One breast looks different from before. Note when you first saw it.

Skin changes

Dimpling, redness, or other unusual changes that do not go away.

Nipple changes

A newly inverted nipple or other unusual change.

Unusual discharge

Especially if bloody. Do not ignore discharge that is new or unexplained.

Do not wait for pain. A new or persistent change should be assessed by a healthcare professional.
